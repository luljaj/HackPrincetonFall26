Names have been corrected where Whisper misheard them ("Zora" = Dora, "Fanny/Spanny" = Benny, "Chico" = Tico, "Jorah" = Dora, "Beagle" = Diego).

## How I rated slots

- **Genuine**: Dora, Boots, Map, Backpack or a helper speaks to the viewer and waits for a spoken or physical reply. Excluded: characters asking each other, travel-song call lines, plain exclamations, and lines where the "wait" is just music.
- **Strong**: the show's next line is generic ("Great!", "I like that too") or happens to confirm the everyday answer, so an everyday challenge fits without breaking continuity.
- **Possible**: works only if the new question still matches the shot (for example a "find it" shot where the place or object stays on screen).
- **Weak**: the next shot depends on the original answer (a specific path, shape, color, Spanish word or object).
- **Inserted slot**: a moment with no native wait (or a non-question gap) where the player could pause anyway because the very next line confirms an everyday answer. These are often the best demo moments, but they aren't counted in the "genuine" totals.

## 3. Cross-episode findings

  1. **07:12** seatbelt challenge (safety), which the show itself confirms with "Seatbelt! So we can be safe!"
  2. **07:51** inserted slot: should stuck Benny jump or wait for the firefighter? (safety / cause-and-effect). The show continues with the ladder rescue.
  3. **08:08** the native "arriba" Spanish prompt, left as-is. This shows that original exercises can coexist with swapped ones.

- Runner-up: Episode 1, about 15:43 to 17:40 (the street crossing at the Nut Farm). It's thematically perfect (crossing-guard signs, holding hands, a 9-second look-both-ways gap at 17:10), but the surrounding rojo/verde questions bind the next lines, so only one or two slots swap cleanly.
- Both scenes rely on inferred visuals. Watch them once to confirm the fire truck boarding and Benny in the tree line up with the timestamps.

**Patterns**
- **Frequency:** 25 to 33 genuine audience pauses per episode, or roughly one every 45 to 50 seconds of story. They cluster around a fixed skeleton:
  - cold-open question, then "Will you help?"
  - "Who do we ask?" / "Say map!", then "Where do we go first?" / "Do you see X?"
  - two obstacles, each with a say-a-word or choose task, with a "Where next?" pair in between
  - a Backpack find, a star catch (eps 2-4), a Swiper stop (eps 2-3)
  - "Favorite part?", then an end-card point-to-it game
- **Wait length:** detected waits have a median of about 3 to 4 s (per-episode medians 4.0 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 3.1 s; most fall between 2 and 5 s). The end-card bumpers run 25 to 30 s, and action prompts ("look both ways", the lullaby) run 9 to 11 s. The AI exchange will need to pause the video rather than fit inside the native gap.
- **What swaps well:** "Will you help...?" yes/no prompts (the show only says "Great!"), open questions (grown-up job, favorite part), and moments where the next line names a safe behavior (seatbelt, waiting for help, warm clothes, umbrella).
- **What doesn't swap well:** "Where next?", Spanish say-a-word, shape/color/path choices and Swiper calls. The following shot depends on the exact answer. Roughly 70% of genuine pauses are Weak.
- **Detector notes:** about 9% false positives (song lines, character-to-character questions, exclamations). It misses prompts whose wait is filled with music or sound effects (the lullaby, the jewel-spotting moment, the snowball yells). Some single asks show up twice because Dora repeats the question ("Can you say salta?" / "Say salta.").
